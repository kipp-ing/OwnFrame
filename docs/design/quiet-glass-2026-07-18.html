Baseline

Where the chrome stands today

The current chrome already does the right things: it hides by default, reveals on a tap, auto-hides after 4.5 s, and on iOS 26 the buttons are real Liquid Glass. The layout — info, albums and settings up top, transport at the bottom — is settled and spec-anchored, and this proposal keeps it.

What makes it feel technical rather than welcoming:

  • The scrims are blunt. Two fixed 45%-black bands fade in over the photo. Over a bright sky they read as smudges; over a dark photo they’re invisible anyway. They solve legibility with darkness instead of with the material.
  • The info card is a data sheet. A calendar icon, a pin icon, a timestamp with minutes — it answers “what are this file’s properties?”, not “where was this?”. And it lives inside the chrome: it dies every time the chrome auto-hides, so it can never be a proper photo-frame caption.
  • The iOS 17 fallback washes out. Plain ultraThinMaterial circles turn near-white over bright photos while their icons stay white — the worst case is the brightest wall in the house.
  • The clock exists everywhere except on screen. It’s in settings, persisted, even remotely controllable from Home Assistant — but nothing renders it. The calmest piece of ambient info the app could show is missing.
Direction

Four ideas carry the whole redesign

01 · Two layers, not one

Chrome is transient, info is ambient

Buttons and transport stay tap-to-reveal and auto-hide, exactly as today. Photo details and the clock move to a separate ambient layer that may stay on screen all evening — though the clock steps aside whenever controls come up. A picture frame has a caption; it doesn’t have toolbars.

02 · One material, three tiers

Glass, soft glass, focus glass

iOS 26+ uses Liquid Glass as-is. iOS 17–25 gets “soft glass”: the same blur with a gentle dark tint baked in, so white symbols always sit on a mid-dark ground. tvOS reuses the same shapes with the focus ring doing the highlighting.

03 · Light by easing, not by bands

Scrims that whisper

The flat 45%-black gradients become eased, longer, and quieter (34% peak), and they fade toward ~15% over photos that are already dark. The glass carries most of the legibility; the scrim only steadies the extremes.

04 · Say it like a person

Captions, not metadata

“Jökulsárlón, Iceland — June 2021” instead of icon-plus-timestamp rows. Rounded type, place first, date without minutes. Settings copy to match: “Photo details”, “Clock”, never “EXIF”.

Photo details

From data sheet to caption

Make it configurable where it matters

Three honest questions — when, what, where — instead of a single on/off that’s coupled to the chrome. All of it lives in the existing Display settings section, replacing the placeholder rows, and every row maps to a Home Assistant entity the same way order, duration and the clock already do.

  • Show — While chrome is open (today’s behavior, the default), Always (true photo-frame captions), or Off.
  • Detail — Place & date, Place only, Date only. Never the filename, never coordinates.
  • Position — caption corner, mirroring the clock’s place picker; the two overlays refuse to share a corner rather than overlap.

Clock

A clock you can read from the sofa

One rule anchors everything: the clock is ambience, the chrome is attention — they never share the screen. The moment controls appear, the clock fades out; when the chrome auto-hides after 4.5 s, it returns. That also dissolves every layout conflict the clock could ever have with buttons, transport or the TV focus pill.

Sized by physics, not by taste

Legibility at distance is arithmetic: comfortable glance reading needs digits at least viewing distance ÷ 125 tall — 12 mm at 1.5 m, so the default targets ~15 mm with margin. iOS points are nearly physical units, which makes the implementation a per-device constant rather than a screen fraction:

ScreenDigit height≈ type size
iPhone at 1.5 m~15 mm≈ 92 pt
iPad at 1.5 m~15 mm≈ 76 pt
Apple TV at 3 m~30 mm on a 55″ panel≈ 90 pt

That is the Room size in the playground — deliberately bigger than any normal UI text, StandBy-style. Cozy (~10 mm) is for desks and nightstands where the frame sits within arm's reach.

Three styles, six places

  • Digits — the default and the photo-frame answer: bare rounded numerals on a soft halo, no box. Big enough to glance from the couch, quiet enough to ignore.
  • Pill — the compact glass capsule, right for within ~1 m; it deliberately fails the sofa test, so it isn't the default.
  • Analog — and yes, a genuinely round clock: a small glass disc with four ticks and two hands. Shape reads faster than digits at distance, and it makes the iPad feel like a shelf clock, not a computer.

On placement — try all six in the playground; the honest recommendation is bottom right as the shipped default. The caption balances it bottom-left, and the bottom edge stays calmer than the top, where chrome buttons appear and muscle memory expects controls. Top middle is the one serious alternative — symmetric, nightstand-like, especially with the analog face. Since it's one settings row (and one Home Assistant select), shipping the picker costs almost nothing; nobody has to be right on day one. Settings also offer Random (agreed): the clock adopts one of the six places and drifts to a new one every few minutes on a photo advance — never mid-photo — indecision as a feature, and extra insurance against burn-in.

If the clock claims the caption's corner, the caption slides to the opposite one — the playground demonstrates this with bottom-left. On tvOS all clock styles drift a few points every few minutes against burn-in, per the TV spec.

Legibility

Readable on white, grey and black alike

The receipt for the two hardest walls. Liquid Glass adapts on its own — over very bright content iOS 26 flips the glass label from white to dark automatically, which the white tiles below emulate. Soft glass never needs to flip: it brings its own mid-dark ground on old devices. And if a pill feels like too much furniture one day, ambient text still survives on a local halo scrim plus a soft shadow — never a hard band.

Apple TV

The same design at ten feet

Nothing on tvOS is tappable, so the transient chrome shrinks to what the Siri Remote can focus: a single paused/resume pill with the system focus ring. The ambient layer is identical to iPad — caption left, clock right — just set ~16% larger.

  • Remote activity reveals chrome; Menu hides it, then exits — as the tvOS spec already defines.
  • The spec’s no-burn-in rule is a design feature here: ambient pills drift a few points every few minutes. Glass with no fixed hairline edge makes that shift invisible.
  • Glass gates on tvOS 26+, soft glass covers tvOS 17+ — the identical two-tier shim the iPhone and iPad use.

Implementation

How the mocks map to the code

The redesign is deliberately a re-skin plus one new renderer — the chrome layout, gestures, timers and accessibility identifiers all stay, so the existing UITest suite keeps guarding it.

Suggested order: soft-glass tier and scrims first (pure re-skin, screenshot-diffable) → caption pill → ambient layer split → clock renderer + live settings rows → tvOS reuse when the 1000 branch lands.

Caveat: these mocks approximate Liquid Glass with CSS blur — the real material refracts and adapts more richly. Next step when this direction feels right: a sim branch that re-skins SlideshowChrome for real and screenshots it over these same photos on the iOS 26.5 and 17.5 simulators.
